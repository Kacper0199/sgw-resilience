1.0 PURPOSE

Sets how broken distribution poles are replaced during storm restoration and when temporary
construction may be used to restore service sooner.

2.0 SCOPE

Wood distribution poles up to 50 ft. Transmission structures are out of scope.

3.0 RESPONSIBILITIES

3.1 Line crew leaders decide between replacement and temporary construction under section 6.

3.2 The district engineer reviews temporary construction records after the event.

3.3 Joint Use Administration notifies third-party attachers.

4.0 DEFINITIONS

Temporary construction: any repair that does not meet the SGW construction standard, including
stub poles, guyed broken poles, conductors on the ground in protected locations, and tree
attachments.

Storm standard pole: Class 3, 40 ft wood pole (Attachment A).

5.0 PRECAUTIONS AND LIMITS

5.1 Do not climb a broken or cracked pole. Use a bucket truck, or top the pole after it is
    secured.

5.2 Call for utility locates before setting a new pole hole unless the hole is in the same
    location as the broken pole.

5.3 Telecom and cable attachments are transferred by their owners. Notify them within 10 working
    days.

5.4 Aerial-lift work stops above 30 mph sustained wind (DOM 14.110).

6.0 PROCEDURE

6.1 Replace

6.1.1 Make the site safe per DOM 14.120.

6.1.2 Take the replacement pole and hardware from yard stock.

6.1.3 Set the new pole beside the broken one where the line allows.

6.1.4 Transfer primary, neutral and secondary. Leave third-party attachments on the old stub
      with a transfer tag.

6.1.5 Record the pole number, class and height on the work order.

6.2 Temporary Construction

6.2.1 Temporary construction may be used when it restores service at least 24 h sooner than a
      full replacement, or when poles are out of stock.

6.2.2 Allowed methods:

a. Stub pole lashed to the broken pole, primary at reduced height not lower than 18 ft over
   roads.

b. Broken pole guyed back and propped where the break is above the primary.

c. Secondary on the ground only on private property, inside a fenced area, with the owner told.

d. Primary on a tree only with a tree-attachment insulator and for no more than 14 days.

6.2.3 Tag every temporary site with a yellow storm tag and log it on F-DOM-118 with "TEMP" in
      remarks.

6.2.4 Temporary construction shall be made permanent within 90 days of the all-clear.

CAUTION: no temporary construction over a road, rail line or waterway without minimum ground
         clearance.

6.3 Follow-up

6.3.1 The district engineer reviews TEMP entries weekly until all are closed.

6.3.2 Rebuild crews close each TEMP entry with the permanent work order number.

6.4 Stock and Replenishment

6.4.1 The Staging Area Manager reports pole counts by class to Logistics at 0600 and 1800 EDT.

6.4.2 When a yard falls below half of the Attachment A quantity for any class, Logistics orders
      a replenishment load (MAL 05.050).

6.4.3 Poles left over after the event are returned to the district yard and counted.

6.4.4 Broken poles are cut into lengths under 10 ft at the site only where the property owner
      agrees. Otherwise haul them to the yard.

7.0 RECORDS

Work orders, F-DOM-118 TEMP entries, transfer tags. Retain 5 years.

8.0 REFERENCES

DOM 14.110. DOM 14.120. GEP 2T.01. GEP 2W.01. National Electrical Safety Code Rule 232 (vertical
clearances).

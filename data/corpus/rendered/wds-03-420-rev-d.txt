1.0 PURPOSE

This procedure sets how mains are flushed from the source outward after pressure in a zone fell
below the state floor, before sampling to lift a boil water advisory or notice.

2.0 SCOPE

Applies to all SGW water systems after a low-pressure event, a main break repair, or a zone
isolation. Routine unidirectional flushing programs follow the annual flushing plan.

3.0 RESPONSIBILITIES

3.1 Water Incident Lead: approves the flushing sequence and orders valve operations.

3.2 Flushing Crew Leader: runs the sequence and keeps the flushing record.

3.3 Water Quality Technician: reads residual and turbidity at each hydrant and draws
    bacteriological samples per WQC 07.130.

3.4 Water Compliance Officer: confirms the zone is ready for rescission sampling.

4.0 DEFINITIONS

Directional (unidirectional) flushing: closing valves so water moves one way, from a clean
source toward the hydrant, at scouring velocity. Pipe volume: the volume of the main segment
between the open source valve and the flushing hydrant.

5.0 PRECAUTIONS AND LIMITS

5.1 Do not start flushing until pressure in the zone has been at or above the state floor for 1
    h and the source tank or station is stable.

5.2 Hydrant discharge shall not flood a roadway or private property. Use diffusers.

5.3 Dechlorinate per Attachment A where discharge can reach a stream or storm inlet.

5.4 CAUTION: Open and close hydrants and valves slowly, over at least 60 s, to avoid water
    hammer.

5.5 State floors: SC 25 psi at service connections in normal operation and 20 psi under heavy
    flow (SC R.61-58.7.E(7)); GA 20 psi at all times (Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 391-3-5-.10).

6.0 PROCEDURE

6.1 Plan the sequence

6.1.1 Mark the affected area from the pressure records (WDS 03.105).

6.1.2 On the valve map, draw flushing runs from the station or tank outward. Larger mains first.

6.1.3 List the valves to close and the hydrant for each run.

6.1.4 Get the Water Incident Lead's approval of the sequence.

6.2 Prepare

6.2.1 Notify customers on the run by door hanger if flushing will discolor water for more than 2
      h.

6.2.2 Check that the hydrant drains away from traffic.

6.2.3 Calculate the pipe volume and the flow needed for 2.5 ft/s from Attachment B.

6.3 Flush each run

6.3.1 Close valves on the order. Record them on F-WDS-212.

6.3.2 Open the hydrant fully. Measure flow with a pitot gauge.

6.3.3 Flush at least 3 pipe volumes at 2.5 ft/s or more.

6.3.4 Read free chlorine and turbidity every 10 min after the first pipe volume.

6.3.5 Stop when free chlorine matches the source within 0.2 mg/L and turbidity is below 1.0 NTU
      for two readings.

6.3.6 Close the hydrant slowly. Open the run's valves on the order.

6.3.7 Move to the next run.

6.4 After flushing

6.4.1 Report completed runs to the Water Compliance Officer.

6.4.2 Bacteriological sampling for rescission follows WQC 07.114 (Georgia) or WQC 07.120 (South
      Carolina) and the site list in WQC 07.130.

7.0 RECORDS

Flushing record (runs, volumes, readings) and F-WDS-212. Retain 10 years with the advisory file.

8.0 REFERENCES

WDS 03.105; WDS 03.240; WDS 03.410; WQC 07.114; WQC 07.120; WQC 07.130; WQC 07.140.

1.0 PURPOSE

Sets how temporary protective grounds are applied during storm restoration so that workers are
protected from accidental energizing, induced voltage and backfeed.

2.0 SCOPE

All de-energized overhead and substation work on SGW lines and equipment during storm
restoration, by SGW, mutual-aid and contract crews. Mutual-aid crews follow this procedure, not
their home rules, while working on the SGW system (briefed under DOM 12.040).

3.0 RESPONSIBILITIES

3.1 The clearance holder (TSO 06.110) decides grounding locations and keeps the ground log.

3.2 Crew members apply and remove grounds as directed by the clearance holder.

3.3 The Staging Area Manager issues inspected ground sets from the yard.

4.0 DEFINITIONS

Equipotential zone (EPZ): grounding arranged so that a worker is never in series with a path for
fault current.

Ground set: clamps, ferrules and cable rated for the available fault current.

Backfeed: voltage returning from the secondary side, often from customer-owned portable
generators.

5.0 PRECAUTIONS AND LIMITS

5.1 Ground only after a clearance has been issued and a Hot Line Hold or open point is in place.

5.2 Test for absence of voltage with a tested detector immediately before applying grounds.

5.3 Use ground sets rated for the location per Attachment A.

5.4 Do not use a ground set that has a broken strand at the ferrule, a cracked clamp or missing
    inspection tag.

5.5 Attach the ground end first; remove it last. Use a hot stick for all phase connections.

CAUTION: storm damage creates crossed circuits and customer generator backfeed. Treat every
         conductor as energized until grounded.

6.0 PROCEDURE

6.1 Before Grounding

6.1.1 Confirm the clearance and every open point by three-part communication with the switching
      desk.

6.1.2 Hold a tailboard. Name the grounding locations and the worker who applies each set.

6.2 Apply

6.2.1 Ground at the work location to build an EPZ: bracket the work, or ground at the pole being
      worked with a cluster bar.

6.2.2 Ground both sides of the work when backfeed is possible, including any section with
      services that could carry customer generator power.

6.2.3 Connect the ground end to the system neutral, cluster bar or station ground grid.

6.2.4 Connect phases nearest to farthest.

6.2.5 Record each ground on the ground log: location, set number, time applied.

6.3 Remove

6.3.1 Confirm all workers are clear.

6.3.2 Remove phase ends farthest to nearest, then the ground end.

6.3.3 Record the removal time. The clearance holder counts sets back before releasing the
      clearance.

6.3.4 Return sets to the yard. Damaged sets are tagged out of service.

6.4 Special Cases

6.4.1 Pad-mounted transformers and underground cable: ground at the elbow with a feed-through
      bushing and a grounding elbow rated per Attachment A.

6.4.2 Substations: connect to the station ground grid at the tagged grounding points only.

6.4.3 Long spans in parallel with an energized line: expect induced voltage. Ground at each end
      of the section and at the work location.

6.4.4 Wire on the ground: ground the conductor on both sides of the break before cutting (DOM
      14.120).

7.0 RECORDS

Ground logs are kept with the clearance record for 3 years.

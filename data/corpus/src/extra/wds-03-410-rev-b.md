# 1.0 PURPOSE

This procedure sets the triage, isolation, repair and return-to-service steps for water main breaks found during and after storms.

# 2.0 SCOPE

Applies to distribution mains 2 in. and larger in all SGW water systems while an event is declared under GEP 00.010. Routine breaks outside declared events follow the district repair practice.

# 3.0 RESPONSIBILITIES

3.1 Water Incident Lead: sets repair priority across districts; orders every valve isolation.

3.2 Water Branch Director or district supervisor: assigns repair crews and reports progress each operational period.

3.3 Water Compliance Officer: decides on boil water advisories or notices with the Water Incident Lead.

3.4 Repair Crew Foreman: makes the site safe, isolates on order, repairs and flushes.

# 4.0 DEFINITIONS

Uprooted-tree break: a main or service torn out by a falling tree root ball. Integrity loss: any break that drops pressure below the state floor or admits soil or water into the main.

# 5.0 PRECAUTIONS AND LIMITS

5.1 Crews shall not drive through water over a roadway (GEP 2F.01).

5.2 Where a tree is tangled in overhead conductors, no excavation starts until electric make-safe is confirmed per DOM 14.120.

5.3 Trench work deeper than 5 ft requires a protective system.

5.4 State pressure floors: South Carolina 25 psi at service connections in normal operation and 20 psi under heavy flow (SC R.61-58.7.E(7)); Georgia 20 psi at all times (Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 391-3-5-.10).

# 6.0 PROCEDURE

## 6.1 Report and triage

6.1.1 Log every reported break with location, size, and time found.

6.1.2 Rank the break by Attachment A.

6.1.3 Report Priority A breaks to the Water Incident Lead at once.

## 6.2 Make safe

6.2.1 Set traffic control.

6.2.2 Check for downed or tangled conductors. If present, request electric make-safe and wait.

6.2.3 Mark underground utilities. Call 811 with the emergency ticket type.

## 6.3 Isolation

6.3.1 Identify the valves on the valve map. Count the customers and critical facilities inside the shutdown.

6.3.2 Request the isolation from the Water Incident Lead. Give the valve numbers.

6.3.3 Close valves only after the order. Record each valve, turns and time on F-WDS-212.

6.3.4 Notify any hospital or dialysis center inside the shutdown before closing.

## 6.4 Pressure and water quality

6.4.1 Read pressure at the nearest hydrant before and after isolation.

6.4.2 If pressure at any service connection fell below the state floor, notify the Water Compliance Officer within 1 h.

6.4.3 The boil water evaluation then follows WQC 07.114 (Georgia) or WQC 07.120 (South Carolina).

## 6.5 Repair and return to service

6.5.1 Keep the trench dewatered below the pipe.

6.5.2 Swab new fittings and pipe with 1 percent hypochlorite solution.

6.5.3 Repair with a full-circle clamp or a cut-in sleeve. Replace a section where the barrel is split more than 12 in.

6.5.4 Open valves slowly on the Water Incident Lead's order.

6.5.5 Flush per WDS 03.420 until free chlorine residual matches the zone.

6.5.6 Take bacteriological samples where WQC 07.130 requires them.

# 7.0 RECORDS

Break log, F-WDS-212 isolation record and repair sheet. Retain 10 years.

# 8.0 REFERENCES

GEP 00.010; GEP 2F.01; DOM 14.120; WDS 03.105; WDS 03.420; WQC 07.114; WQC 07.120; WQC 07.130.

# ATTACHMENT A BREAK TRIAGE

| Priority | Condition | Target crew assignment |
|---|---|---|
| A | Break in a hospital zone, transmission main 12 in. and up, or loss below state floor | Within 2 h |
| B | Break with 250 or more customers affected or a road undermined | Within 8 h |
| C | Other mains, services leaking to surface | Within 24 h |
| D | Leaks with no service impact | After the event |

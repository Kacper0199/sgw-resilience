# 1.0 PURPOSE

Sets how SGW responds to downed wires during storms and how a wire is made safe before restoration.

# 2.0 SCOPE

All overhead distribution conductors, service drops and communication cables reported down in the SGW
electric service area. Transmission conductors down follow TSO 06.110 and the transmission patrol instructions.

# 3.0 RESPONSIBILITIES

3.1 The wire guard dispatch desk (DOM 14.105 Attachment A) assigns wire guards and keeps the downed-wire queue.

3.2 Wire guards stand by the wire, keep the public away and report conditions. Wire guards do not touch,
cut or ground conductors.

3.3 Make-safe crews (qualified line workers) de-energize, cut, clear and ground wires.

3.4 The Duty Manager sets the queue priority when calls exceed wire guard capacity.

# 4.0 DEFINITIONS

Downed wire: any conductor or cable on the ground, within reach, or in contact with a structure, tree or
vehicle.
Make-safe: the wire is isolated from all sources, cut clear or tied off, and the site is released.
Wire guard: a trained SGW or contract employee assigned to watch a downed wire. Wire guards are not line
qualified.

# 5.0 PRECAUTIONS AND LIMITS

5.1 Treat every downed wire as energized until a make-safe crew has tested and grounded it.

5.2 Keep the public at least 35 ft from the wire and anything it touches. Wire guards stay at least 35 ft
away themselves.

5.3 Do not drive over a downed wire. Do not park under a damaged span.

5.4 A wire in water, on a fence or on a vehicle with occupants is a Priority A call.

CAUTION: voltage spreads through wet ground. Approach is by shuffling steps only for qualified crews with
the required PPE.

# 6.0 PROCEDURE

## 6.1 Intake

6.1.1 Log each downed-wire report in OMS with the source (911, fire, police, customer, crew).

6.1.2 Classify the call by Attachment A.

6.1.3 Calls from 911 centers and fire departments go to the top of their priority class.

## 6.2 Wire Guard Dispatch

6.2.1 Dispatch a wire guard to every Priority A and B call.

6.2.2 The target is a wire guard on site within 60 min of a 911-reported call.

6.2.3 On arrival the wire guard reports the pole number or nearest address, the wire type if known, and
whether anything is in contact.

6.2.4 The wire guard sets cones and tape at 35 ft and stays until relieved by a make-safe crew or released by
the dispatch desk.

## 6.3 Make-Safe

6.3.1 Identify the source. Open the feeding device and apply a Hot Line Hold where switching is used (TSO
06.110).

6.3.2 Test for absence of voltage.

6.3.3 Apply temporary protective grounds per TSO 06.130 before cutting or handling.

6.3.4 Cut the wire clear of the roadway or tie it off at the pole.

6.3.5 Record the make-safe time on the OMS ticket. The site is then released to the restoration crew or
closed.

6.3.6 Remove grounds and release the Hot Line Hold only when the restoration crew is clear.

## 6.4 Fire and Police Standby

Fire and police may hold a wire site in place of a wire guard. Record the agency and unit on the OMS ticket.
Relieve them within 2 h or ask the Duty Manager for a priority change.

# 7.0 RECORDS

OMS downed-wire tickets. Wire guard log sheets. Retain 3 years.

# 8.0 REFERENCES

DOM 14.105. DOM 14.110. TSO 06.110. TSO 06.130. GEP 2T.01. 29 CFR 1910.269(n) (grounding for employee
protection).

# ATTACHMENT A - Downed Wire Priority

| Priority | Condition | Wire guard | Make-safe target |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Wire on vehicle with occupants, in water, on fence or structure, arcing, or fire | Immediate | Next available crew |
| B | Wire across road or sidewalk, or reported by 911 | Within 60 min | Within the shift |
| C | Wire in yard or field, not touching anything | When available | Restoration crew handles |
| D | Service drop or communication cable only | Not required | Restoration crew handles |

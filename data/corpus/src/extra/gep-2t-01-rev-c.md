# 1.0 PURPOSE

Adds actions for tornadoes, derechos and severe thunderstorm lines to the base ERP (GEP 00.010).

# 2.0 SCOPE

Applies to all districts. Tornadoes embedded in tropical systems are handled under GEP 2H.01, with section 5
of this annex used for crew shelter.

# 3.0 HAZARD SUMMARY

Severe convective damage is short-notice and concentrated. A single tornado track can break dozens of poles
on one feeder while the next feeder is untouched. Straight-line wind lines (derechos) produce damage closer to
a tropical event. Water impact is mostly loss of power at pump stations and debris on access roads.

# 4.0 TRIGGERS AND ACTIVATION

| Storm Prediction Center (SPC) product | Default Activation Level | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Day 2 Slight or Enhanced Risk over the service area | 5 Normal | Duty Manager reviews on-call depth |
| Day 1 Enhanced Risk, or Severe Thunderstorm Watch | 4 Monitoring | Weather call; districts hold crews to 2100 |
| Day 1 Moderate Risk, or Tornado Watch over two or more districts | 3 Partial Activation | Storm room on 12 h shifts |
| Day 1 High Risk, or confirmed damage path over 10 mi | 2 Elevated Activation | Mutual-aid evaluation |

After the event the IC sets the Event Type from damage reports per GEP 00.010 section 4. Most convective events
are Event Type 3 or 4.

# 5.0 CREW SAFETY

## 5.1 Lightning

5.1.1 Stop work at a structure when lightning is within 10 mi or thunder is heard.

5.1.2 Resume 30 min after the last lightning within 10 mi.

5.1.3 Aerial-lift work stops when sustained wind exceeds 30 mph (DOM 14.110). Gusts in thunderstorm outflow
often exceed this before the rain arrives.

## 5.2 Tornado Warning

5.2.1 The Distribution Control Center broadcasts each NWS Tornado Warning on the district talkgroups within
2 min of issue.

5.2.2 Crews inside or within 5 mi of the warning polygon shall stop work and shelter.

5.2.3 Shelter in a sturdy building, interior room, lowest floor. A vehicle is not shelter. If no building is
reachable, leave the truck and lie flat in the lowest ground away from poles and trees.

5.2.4 Crews report "sheltered" and "clear" by radio. The Duty Manager logs both times.

5.2.5 Crews resume work only after the warning expires and the supervisor has checked the site for new
downed wires.

# 6.0 RESTORATION ACTIONS

## 6.1 First 6 Hours

| Step | Position | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Duty Manager | Map the damage path from OMS outage clusters and 911 reports |
| 2 | Duty Manager | Assign wire guards to downed-wire calls inside the path (DOM 14.120) |
| 3 | Manager, Transmission Operations | Patrol transmission lines crossing the path |
| 4 | Duty Manager | Send damage assessors along the path at 1 assessor per 3 mi |
| 5 | Logistics Section Chief | Order poles, crossarms and transformers to the nearest yard (DOM 14.180, DOM 14.160) |
| 6 | Water Incident Lead | Confirm power and access at pump stations near the path |
| 7 | Planning Section Chief | Brief the IC for the Event Type decision |

## 6.2 Rebuild

6.2.1 Inside a tornado path, plan feeder rebuilds rather than spot repairs. Temporary construction follows
DOM 14.180 section 6.

6.2.2 Restoration priority follows DOM 14.215.

6.2.3 After outages longer than 4 h, re-energize in sections per DOM 14.130.

6.2.4 Where a substation is lost, the Manager, Transmission Operations evaluates a mobile substation per
DOM 14.170.

# 7.0 COMMUNICATIONS

Public messaging follows PCN 02.050. Corporate Communications releases a downed-wire safety message within
1 h of a confirmed tornado touchdown in the service area.

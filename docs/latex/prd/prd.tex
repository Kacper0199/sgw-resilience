\sgwsection{summary}{1.0}{Summary}

\begin{sgwbox}{Problem}
During a storm, SGW decides where crews, fuel and generators go without seeing how much time is left before each decision stops mattering. Power and water decisions that depend on each other are taken on separate desks.
\end{sgwbox}

\textbf{Product.} The \sgwproduct{} is a decision queue for a utility that runs both power and water.
Each open decision carries a deadline (shown on screen as Decide by) with its basis, the chain of actions that sets it, and a timing signal (Act now, Before next advisory, Can wait).
Deadlines are computed by code from model outputs. The AI assistant explains them, and a person signs every action with high impact.
In a footprint of \sgwnum{footprint.residents}, Hurricane Helene left \sgwnum{storm.helene.peak_customers_out} without power at its peak.

\textbf{MVP.} Five decisions in one storm replay. D1 sets the event type, D2 stages crews before landfall, D3 decides pump station power and the boil water notice, D4 publishes the global estimated time of restoration (ETR) with a range, and D5 reviews the event. Unsafe actions, bypassed approvals and invented identifiers stay at zero.
The pilot aims to cut the time from the all clear to the first approved global ETR from \sgwnum{metric.m07.baseline_h} at a peer utility to at most \sgwnum{metric.m07.m12_h}, to keep the median error of the global ETR at most \sgwnum{metric.m06.m12_pct}, and to flag every critical site with its time to empty within \sgwnum{metric.m11.flag_min}. These targets are assumed (Section~\ref{sgw:sec:metrics}).

\textbf{Prototype.} A working prototype replays Hurricane Helene on synthetic SGW data and real public storm data. A scripted exercise on the real track of Hurricane Ian adds \sgwval{scenario.ian.decisions} timed decisions. The code repository delivered with this document holds the prototype with its tests and evaluation reports, and a video walks through it at \url{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zSRUEBEpw7Y}.

\sgwsection{problem}{1.25}{Problem and business context}

\textbf{The baseline story.}
In the Helene replay used throughout this document, pump station PS-17 keeps the pressure zone of a hospital above the state floor of \sgwnum{sc_pressure_floor_psi} \citep{sc_r61_58}.
When the feeder of the station trips, a portable generator must be connected within its lead time of \sgwnum{demo.ps17.generator_lead}, or the zone drains below the floor at the service connection of the hospital.
The fuel truck that keeps the generator running must leave before the roads close, so the chain runs from truck to generator to pressure (Figure~\ref{fig:critpath}).
At one forecast advisory the effective deadline of the generator decision is \clockN{} and the signal says that the decision can wait.
The next advisory moves it earlier, to \clockNone, and the signal turns to Before next advisory, because waiting for one more advisory would be too late.
The consequence time comes from a water model checked against full hydraulic runs, so these are synthetic values, not SGW measurements.
Today no desk sees this deadline. Electric operations sees a feeder, water operations sees a tank, and the hospital calls.

\textbf{Why now.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Storm scale.} Helene left \sgwnum{storm.helene.peak_customers_out} out at the peak in the footprint \citep{doe_eaglei}. Restoring \sgwnum{policy.etr_restore_fraction_pct} of them took \sgwnum{storm.helene.hours_to_90pct} from the peak, against \sgwnum{storm.michael.hours_to_90pct} for Michael and \sgwnum{storm.ian.hours_to_90pct} for Ian. Outage minutes per customer from major events in 2024 were \sgwnum{reliability.major_event_minutes}, against \sgwnum{reliability.major_event_median} in a median year.
  \item \textbf{Scrutiny of storm costs.} The Georgia commission cut a request of Georgia Power to recover storm costs from \sgwnum{context.ga_storm_rev_req_filed_musd} to \sgwnum{context.ga_storm_rev_req_agreed_musd} and opened a review of how storm costs are recorded \citep{ga_psc_44280}.
  \item \textbf{Rules with deadlines.} South Carolina lists the period before landfall as a trigger for a boil water advisory and requires immediate notice \citep{sc_des_bwn}. Interruptions longer than \sgwnum{sc_outage_full_report_hours} need a full report to the commission \citep{sc_psc_103}.
\end{itemize}
GIS, outage, asset, historian, meter, field, laboratory and weather systems each hold part of the picture, and field links fail when they are needed most.

\sgwsection{scope}{0.25}{Goals and out of scope}
\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item[G1] Every open storm decision shows a deadline with its basis (M-14, M-15).
  \item[G2] Dependencies between power and water are visible before the consequence (M-11, M-12).
  \item[G3] Actions with high impact carry two signatures from two functions on one plan version (guardrails).
  \item[G4] The global ETR carries a range and the signature of command (M-06, M-07).
  \item[G5] Every number on screen shows its source, data age and label.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}\par
\textbf{Out of scope.} The platform does not write to SCADA, ADMS switching or any control system, which keeps it advisory and outside the security perimeter. It does not send customer or public notices but drafts them for the certified operator and compliance. There is no public chatbot, because public answers carry legal weight, and no modelling of fire spread or floods, because SGW uses agency products. The MVP has no mobile field app, because field links fail in major storms. Reinforcement learning for dispatch is not built, because too few real storms exist to validate it. It may return as a challenger run in shadow behind a statistical gate.
Beyond the MVP the prototype also holds a what if comparison over a precomputed scenario grid and the triggers and ranking tables for red flag warning days and heat emergencies.

\textbf{How Ask AI answers and is checked.} Ask AI answers questions about the storm in plain words and supports every decision only as a reader. A planner splits the question into tasks for one to three analysts, which read platform data and SGW documents through tools without side effects. The model may not type a number. It writes slots that point to evidence, and code fills in the values. A verifier rejects an answer with an invented number, an unknown identifier or no citation. The writer is then asked to rewrite, and if the answer still fails, code renders the evidence as a short plain answer. Ask AI never drafts, proposes, approves or sends anything, and every step, tool call and rejection is written to the audit trail of the run. The question bank holds \sgwval{eval.askbank.items} questions in five languages.

\textbf{What the evaluations show.} Each of \sgwval{eval.outage.storms_n} real storms was forecast by a model fitted on the other storms only, and richer models tested with a plan fixed in advance did not beat the wind curve. It underestimates large storms, most of all inland storms such as Helene, so phase 1 adds a rainfall forecast and a new test on storms the model has never seen (\url{docs/06-outage-forecast.md}). For crews, a plan that also buys more mutual aid to hedge against the worst scenarios cut the delay from \sgwval{eval.saa.median_plan_delay_mch} to \sgwval{eval.saa.tail_hedge_delay_mch} million customer hours, for \sgwval{eval.saa.tail_hedge_aid_musd} instead of \sgwnum{eval.saa.median_plan_aid_musd} of mutual aid.

\textbf{Alternatives considered.}
A dashboard and chatbot over a data lake was rejected, because it shows data but not the time by which a decision must be taken, and authority stays unrecorded.
Extending the suite of the outage vendor was rejected for the MVP, because it covers electric operations only and leaves the decisions between power and water out.

\textbf{Model hosting.} Production reaches a managed Claude model through the existing cloud provider of SGW, behind the gateway, and adds a second zone, a separate evidence store and a monitoring stack.

\sgwsection{data}{0.5}{Data requirements and quality}
Table~\ref{tab:sources} lists each dataset with its owner and prototype source. Each dataset has an owner, a freshness rule and a provenance label (real, derived or synthetic). Phase 0 depends on read access to the outage and meter history of two past storms, the first decision asked of SGW in the summary, and on the register of critical customers (A-013).

\textbf{Quality rules.} Every feed is checked against its schema on arrival. Each record carries a quality flag (good, suspect, bad or missing), its coverage and its data age. Every ingest run and every check is recorded for the Health page. Signals from one physical cause count as one piece of evidence.

\textbf{What the AI model may see.} Data classes run from C0 (public) to C4 (most sensitive). The model receives views of the evidence in which telemetry values stay in slots and never enter the prompt, so those prompts are class C1. C3 and C4 content, such as ranked lists of asset risk and the network topology, never reaches an external model, and the gateway denies a call whose class header does not match its content.

\textbf{Position under NERC CIP.} SGW is assumed to be a NERC registered entity. The platform is decision support outside any electronic security perimeter and not a cyber system of the bulk electric system (BES), which matches the mode that NERC calls decision making support \citep{nerc_aiml_2024}. It reads control system data only through feeds that flow in one direction from an existing historian in the DMZ of the network, and it has no write path back \citep{nerc_cip}. Asset risk rankings are class C3, and if the CIP owner of SGW rules otherwise, the change of scope becomes a gate item in phase 0.

\textbf{Other regulation.} The pressure floors are \sgwnum{sc_pressure_floor_psi} in South Carolina \citep{sc_r61_58} and \sgwnum{ga_pressure_floor_psi} at all times in Georgia \citep{ga_391_3_5_10}, and storm decision records feed the risk and resilience assessment and the emergency response plan of each water system \citep{epa_awia_2013}.

\textbf{NIST AI Risk Management Framework.} The four functions of the framework \citep{nist2023airmf} map as follows. Govern rests on the approval policy, named owners and the kill switch, Map names the decision, data class and failure mode of each capability, Measure uses reserved test sets, backtests on unseen storms and guardrails at zero, and Manage uses degraded modes, templates and the learning loop.
